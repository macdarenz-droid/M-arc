Lateral Raise Lab

The dumbbell lateral raise on a new engine. One continuous 3D body lifts your 7 kg dumbbells. The shoulder blade, elbow, breath, balance and grip all move the way a real body does. Working muscles shimmer on the skin as they fire. You can turn the figure, zoom in, and play the common mistake beside the right rep.

    What moves, besides the arm

    A good rep is the whole body working together. This version animates each of these from the exercise's data.

    • Shoulder bladeAbove 30°, it turns about 1° for every 2° the arm rises. It rotates, it doesn't shrug.
    • Elbows leadThe soft bend deepens by 6° near the top, so the elbow stays just above the hand.
    • BreathingBreathe out on the way up and in on the way down. The chest shows it.
    • BalanceThe body leans back about half a degree at the ankles as the weights move out, with a slow natural sway.
    • GripFingers wrap the handle, and the dumbbells stay level from bottom to top.
    • Holding the topA tiny tremor under load at the top, a little larger on the last rep.
    • TiringEach rep lifts a little slower: rep 2 takes 8% longer, rep 3 takes 18% longer. The lowering stays at 2 s.
    • The mistakeA knee dip and hip drive to throw the weight up, a shrug, the thumbs turning down, then a drop that swings past the start and bounces.

    How it's built

    1. Motion description

      Joint angles over time, the plane, the grip, the tempo, the movement details above, the cues, and the common mistake as the same exercise done wrong.

    2. Shared skeleton

      Standard body proportions (1.78 m), plus a second joint for the shoulder blade, soft knees and ankles for balance.

    3. One continuous body

      Smooth skin runs from the shoulder through the elbow to the wrist, and from the hip through the knee to the ankle, with no ball joints. A tank top leaves the shoulders bare so you can see them work. Fingers wrap the handle.

    4. Muscles shimmer on the skin

      Each muscle's area of skin tints in its colour, and a sheen runs along its fibres from where it starts to where it attaches. The sheen is brighter and faster the harder the muscle works. The body's shape never changes.

    5. Load and speed

      Load is weight × gravity × the sideways distance to the dumbbell. Speed comes from the dumbbell's path, frame to frame.

    6. Automatic checks

      These run before an exercise ships:

      • smooth speed;
      • joints stay in their normal range;
      • hands stay on the handle and feet stay planted;
      • muscle lengths stay in range;
      • the mistake really differs on every fault it names.

    Costs and risks
    
        
      
          
    • Size: the 3D library (three.js, MIT licence) is 150 KB compressed (measured), loaded only when a guide opens. Each exercise adds about 1 KB.
      • 
          
    • Phone speed and battery: the body is rebuilt every frame on the graphics chip. This needs a check on your phone. The fallback is the four stills.
      • 
          
    • No 3D support: older phones get the stills and the same text.
      • 
          
    • Accuracy: the motion and the muscle areas follow published anatomy and studies. They are modelled, not motion-captured, and each new muscle needs its area checked.
      • 
          
    • Rework: the flat rig built today would be retired.
