The dumbbell lateral raise, drawn in a bold comic-anatomy style and painted in the app's own colours. You see the figure as you'd see yourself in the gym mirror.     What moves, besides the arm

    A good rep is the whole body working together. Each of these is animated from the exercise's data.

    • Shoulder bladeAbove 30° the shoulders lift slightly as the blades rotate, about 1° for every 2° the arm rises. It isn't a shrug.
    • Elbows leadNear the top the hands sit just below the elbows.
    • BreathingBreathe out on the way up and in on the way down. The ribcage shows it.
    • BalanceA slow, natural sway, and a tiny settle as the weights go out.
    • GripThe dumbbells stay level from bottom to top.
    • Holding the topA small tremor under load at the top, a little larger on the last rep.
    • TiringEach rep lifts a little slower: rep 2 takes 8% longer, rep 3 takes 18% longer. The lowering stays at 2 s.
    • The mistakeA knee dip to throw the weight up, a shrug, the thumbs turning down, then a drop that swings past the start and bounces.

    How it's built

    1. Motion description

      Joint angles over time, the tempo, the movement details above, the cues, and the common mistake as the same exercise done wrong.

    2. One drawn figure per view

      The figure is drawn once, in parts: head, traps, chest, abs, each arm and each leg. Every part is shaded in three flat tones with a bold outline. Exercises move the parts; they don't redraw them. This sample uses the front view. A side view and a back view are drawn once, and then every exercise reuses them.

    3. Painted by the theme

      The body takes the app's accent colour, with lighter and darker tones worked out from it. Each theme repaints it: lavender in Silent Black, blue in Paper, and the accent of Ember, Emerald or Midnight.

    4. Muscles shimmer

      A working muscle takes on the app's warning colour. A band of light runs along its fibres, brighter and faster the harder it works. The upper trap stays green while it's quiet and turns red when it takes over. The body's shape never changes.

    5. Load and speed

      Load is weight × gravity × the sideways distance to the dumbbell. Speed comes from the dumbbell's path.

    6. Automatic checks

      These run before an exercise ships: smooth speed, joints in their normal range, hands on the handle, feet planted, and a mistake that really differs on every fault it names.

    Costs and risks
    
        
      
          
    • Size: plain SVG with no library. The whole figure is a few KB, and each exercise adds about 1 KB of data.
      • 
          
    • Art: the figure has to be drawn once per view (front, side, back). Exercises seen from above or at an angle would need one more view each.
      • 
          
    • Turning: a drawn figure can't be turned freely the way the 3D version could. You get the view that suits each exercise, plus a close-up.
      • 
          
    • Phone: SVG animation is light. A quick check on your phone is still worth doing.
      • 
          
    • Accuracy: the motion is modelled from anatomy and studies, not motion-captured.
