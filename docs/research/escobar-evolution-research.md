# Part 2 — Escobar evolution

**Date:** 1 October 2026. **Source snapshot:** `d5ebc771b3194dc557d469e40945dfa2ddf1f8ab` (main verified at the start of this research). **Scope:** research, source QA and proposed acceptance criteria only.

This report builds on the separate [original audit](https://github.com/macdarenz-droid/M-arc/blob/7596ee385fe622b8f4635146368505644960c352/codex-audit.md) and [improvement audit](https://github.com/macdarenz-droid/M-arc/blob/83463cf0cdf1f93c57a06a4933267318f2cfa31e/improvement-audit.md). Existing findings keep their original IDs and evidence dates. Another builder owns implementation. No application, repository-root file, branch, dependency, configuration or deployment was changed for this research. The documents are saved outside the checkout.

**Recommendation:** build a dependable daily decision cycle—understand intent, compare feasible options, apply the intended change, and review the result. Escobar already uses a foundation model and a substantial tool catalogue; stronger context, execution and evidence checks are essential to make that capability useful.

The companion Part 1 report contains the training/behavior evidence ledger and shared adaptation requirements. This report separates source-verified capabilities, unfinished connections and proposed upgrades. It makes no GPT/Claude parity, live-model quality, physical-outcome or measured performance claim.

## Main finding

Escobar already has much of the machinery of a useful gym assistant: training-data tools, charts, equipment-aware targets, app navigation, a deterministic programme evaluator, editable application state through proposals, and persistent memories. Its main opportunity is to connect those capabilities into a dependable recurring coaching cycle: understand the person's intent, select a feasible next action, help execute it, then examine the result. More chat intelligence alone does not produce that cycle.

The current experience is predominantly a user-initiated assistant. The daily AI brief, proactive moments and automatic conversation summaries described in the architecture are not wired as production features in this snapshot. The Hall falls back to the local brain's top insight. Voice and video are not present. These distinctions matter more than a claim that it rivals a general-purpose chatbot.

The architecture document begins with “Status: design only.” Much of it subsequently shipped, but its promises cannot serve as proof that each capability exists. In particular, do not repeat its claims of daily generation, episodic memory, editable memory, voice, or graded live-model evaluations without tracing their callers. [Architecture status](https://github.com/macdarenz-droid/M-arc/blob/d5ebc771b3194dc557d469e40945dfa2ddf1f8ab/docs/ESCOBAR-ARCHITECTURE.md#L1-L6).

### Verification boundary

The numeric verifier is useful but narrower than its name may suggest. It checks whether a number appears anywhere in the fact ledger or user messages, with rounding/absolute-value and unit allowances, and excludes some small integers, dates/times, quoted text and set-by-rep patterns. It does **not** prove that a sentence names the correct exercise, time period, unit, sign, cause or person, nor does it require every personal number's cited fact to semantically entail the claim. An old number from another metric can pass membership. A plausible non-numeric explanation can also be wrong. After one unsuccessful repair, flagged sentences are still rendered with an “Unverified number” annotation. [src/escobar/verify.ts:127-162](https://github.com/macdarenz-droid/M-arc/blob/d5ebc771b3194dc557d469e40945dfa2ddf1f8ab/src/escobar/verify.ts#L127-L162); [src/escobar/loop.ts:530-547](https://github.com/macdarenz-droid/M-arc/blob/d5ebc771b3194dc557d469e40945dfa2ddf1f8ab/src/escobar/loop.ts#L530-L547); [src/escobar/ui/Message.tsx:70-90](https://github.com/macdarenz-droid/M-arc/blob/d5ebc771b3194dc557d469e40945dfa2ddf1f8ab/src/escobar/ui/Message.tsx#L70-L90).

The proposal validator is a different layer: it checks supported shapes/ranges, looks up real exercise IDs and rejects blocking programme issues. Apply additionally checks a touched-state fingerprint and expiry. That is valuable executable protection, but a fingerprint covers only the dependencies explicitly listed for its action. The improvement audit's IMP-E01–E05 demonstrate that current execution/context/Undo paths need stronger shared state and identity guarantees before scope expands. ### Privacy boundary

The online coach is opt-in. Specific body and health read tools/components have sharing gates, and historical tool results/briefs are scrubbed when those gates turn off. However, prior assistant/user prose continues to travel with the conversation; disabling sharing does not erase words already said. The brief sends readiness and recovery derived from local data even when raw health sharing is off, as documented in existing decisions. Persistent memory is also distinct from conversation history; resetting conversations deliberately preserves memory.

For expanded adaptation, specify which new observations are computed locally, which are stored, which are sent, their lifetime, and how the person corrects them. Existing schema/provider/paid-call approval rules remain builder responsibilities; this research proposes the shape without authorizing new collection or spending. ## Product target

Escobar should become the easiest way to make and carry out a good training decision in M/ARC. Its advantage should be continuity: it knows the user's chosen plan, understands a change in circumstances, can show a feasible alternative, applies the right change and checks what happened later. The app should retain reliable logging and local guidance without network access or an online coach.

“Rival GPT or Claude” needs two distinct tests. **General reasoning superiority is not established and cannot be promised by an app roadmap. Domain usefulness is testable.** The checked-in Worker already builds on Claude. Escobar can aim to outperform a general chat experience on a person's gym tasks by combining that model capability with better current evidence, constrained tools and less manual work. Any comparison must control for available information and tools.

The conversation features should serve recurring jobs:

| Recurring job | Proposed Escobar experience | Why a user returns |
|---|---|---|
| Decide today's training | A short answer based on the user's actual intent and today's constraints, with one feasible action | Less indecision before training |
| Adapt when life changes | “Shorten this,” “the rack is busy,” “I missed Tuesday,” “different gym today” produces a precise preview | Less manual plan editing and fewer contradictory instructions |
| Finish the session well | Explain the next meaningful adjustment from valid current state without interrupting a set | Useful help at the point of action |
| Understand progress | Distinguish real progress, inconsistent comparison, sparse evidence and a plausible next experiment | Better decisions than a generic motivational recap |
| Continue a decision | Remember the accepted plan change and review its execution at the next relevant opportunity | The user does not have to retell the same context |
| Learn enough to act | A concise explanation grounded in reviewed knowledge and personal facts, with uncertainty where needed | Increasing user competence and trust |

Daily availability does not require a daily unsolicited message. An intentional rest day with no relevant change may need no intervention. The behavioral evidence in Part 1 supports evaluating benefit and burden together; it does not establish that extra chat creates long-term lifting gains. [GPTCoach](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/2025/Joerke_GPTCoach.pdf) supplies feasibility evidence, while the [chatbot RCT synthesis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40656859/) has limited transfer to an LLM gym coach.

## Feature sequence

**E0 — trust foundation.** Finish shared context, operation-bound Apply/Undo, current clock/load/period facts, cancellation and quota admission. Add semantic claim checks and a repeatable evaluation harness. Proposed prescriptions cannot become more autonomous while the same state still produces conflicting targets. Include the newly identified live-watch wiring gap and feature-discovery descriptions in source QA.

E0 also depends on the original audit's science/data findings: assisted-recovery direction, sleep baselines, soreness precedence, readiness across modes, substitution estimates and plan/logged volume (SCI-01/02/03/04/08/09), plus history/import integrity and the improvement audit's replay/provenance defects. A correctly grounded answer can still repeat an incorrect underlying calculation. Referential checks can bind a claim to its metric, entity, time and unit; they cannot establish physiological truth or prove causality. Those require evidence review and appropriate human/adversarial evaluation.

**E1 — daily decision and session fit.** Complete the existing brief path with a meaningful trigger and invalidation policy. Ask for today's time/equipment constraint only when needed. Offer a short-session replan that protects completed work and the user's priority. Generate on foreground events first; a scheduled background service is a separate decision. A cached brief must declare itself obsolete when relevant history, goal, schedule, gym or check-in changes.

The duration planner should respect explicit rest constraints and applicable product rules. Current goal rest defaults are advisory starting points, not experimentally established hard physiological minima. Offer tradeoffs or admit infeasibility instead of silently reducing rest to claim a plan fits.

**E2 — week repair and review.** Negotiate the remaining week using actual available opportunities. Make a review answer “what changed, what matters for your goal, and what to keep or change.” A missed log may warrant a question, not an accusation. Limit the first useful review to one supported change, with unchanged plan always allowed.

**E3 — durable continuity.** Implement reliable memory correction and summaries plus a decision follow-up record. Separate user facts, assistant suggestions, accepted agreements and measured outcomes. Show explicit versus inferred status; scope and expiry must survive summary compression. A new explicit preference supersedes an old inference. A stale injury note should be reviewable, not silently become permanent clearance or a new diagnosis.

**E4 — lower input burden and research.** Only after the above works, test voice logging during rest, structured programme import, and improved equipment-photo understanding. All extracted quantities require a reviewable interpretation and the same validation as typed data. Consider a carefully curated evidence-retrieval service for genuinely unanswered questions; keep personal data out of external search terms by default. General web browsing, nutrition tracking, video form assessment and external calendars are separate expansions, not implied current capabilities.

This order prioritizes recurring user value over the number of tools. It is an inference from current gaps, not proof of product-market fit.

## Proposed experience

The following are proposed upgrades, not verified current features. They are designed to turn Escobar into the part of M/arc that knows what matters today and reduces effort for the user. Their value is a hypothesis to test with users, not a promised adherence or fitness effect.

| Priority | Upgrade | Concrete behavior | Conditions for release |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Shared coaching facts | Today, Train, Body, History and Escobar all read the same target/recovery/schedule snapshot, with units, timestamps and provenance | Repair improvement-audit target, day-off, historical replay, pause and Undo defects first; cross-surface parity suite |
| First | Goal agreement | “Get stronger, three days, 40 minutes, avoid overhead work, prefer dumbbells” becomes a reviewable current goal/constraint set, with explicit versus inferred status | Ask only for missing information that changes the plan; person can correct or revoke any preference; don't infer injury or diagnoses from behavior |
| Next | Daily decision | One relevant choice: normal session, shorter option, move it, or rest; explain the top reason and uncertainty | Generated on meaningful app events/open, with a local fallback; no repeated generic message, no pressure on an intentional rest day |
| Next | Session copilot | “Only 20 minutes left” produces a revised remaining session, retaining logged work and key goal-relevant movements; unfamiliar equipment asks about usable load | Read actual active state, pause clock and gym; enforce remaining-time/equipment constraints; Apply affects remaining work only |
| Next | Recovery conversation | When self-report and estimate disagree, ask the one input that changes the decision; show that recovery is estimated | No single wearable score declares somebody safe or injured; stale/no-watch data gets lower confidence; warm-up/performance can inform a bounded adjustment |
| Next | Week repair | Missed Tuesday or gym closure yields two feasible alternatives for remaining days; compare time, preferences and recovery tradeoffs | Preserve completed history and prior commitments; no catch-up punishment or automatic doubling; invalid alternatives are rejected locally |
| Next | Progress review | “Bench has stalled” compares genuinely comparable exposures, distinguishes missing/edited data, effort/rest, load availability and plan execution; recommends one testable next change | Separate association from cause; no invented “because”; if evidence cannot discriminate, ask or abstain |
| Later | Learning journal | Track agreed change → implementation → next comparable outcome → keep/revise; carry compact summaries across chats | Summaries preserve explicit constraints and uncertainty; no silent promotion of suggestions to user facts; corrections invalidate descendants |
| Later | Scenario comparison | Compare “two days versus three” or “30 minutes versus 60” through a deterministic constrained evaluator; show tradeoffs before Apply | Simulate feasible workload/time only; do not predict precise muscle gain or injury probability without validated models |
| Later | Easier input | Optional short voice command during rest and reliable equipment-photo confirmation; extract one observation into a proposal | New provider/data costs reviewed separately; noisy gym, accents, kg/lb and false numbers evaluated; no inference of safe form from one photo |

A useful sample interaction is: “You have 35 minutes and today is your second of three planned sessions. Keep the main lift and two accessories. Your last session's load is available here; your check-in suggests leaving more reps in reserve. Apply this shorter version?” The values and reasoning must come from verified tools, not an example template copied regardless of evidence. If the person says “just log my workouts,” Escobar should remain quiet and help on demand.

## Reasoning design

Use **deterministic training decisions plus model judgment and language**, with a clear boundary between them. The foundation model handles free-form intent, questions, tradeoff explanations and choosing which evidence to request. Local code owns arithmetic, data eligibility, identity, physical equipment loads, hard constraints, executable changes and verification. “High effort” or a longer prompt does not replace these guarantees.

A proposed decision record should contain the user's request, explicit current goal and constraints, source snapshot/version, supporting observations and time window, missing information, candidate actions, why rejected candidates fail, proposed action, and review condition. This is an auditable result and brief rationale, not a request to expose a model's hidden chain of thought. Most of it can remain internal; the user sees the answer, the decisive reason and one useful action.

For each turn: classify the job and risk; obtain current facts; distinguish hard constraints from soft preferences; ask a clarification only when it changes feasibility; generate a small candidate set; score feasible candidates against the person's priorities; validate structured factual claims; render the right card; commit through a validated command only after the appropriate user action; reevaluate if the underlying state changed. This sequence can use one model with local evaluators; multi-agent fan-out is not required for ordinary gym questions. The design draws on [Anthropic’s workflow guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents) and [context-engineering guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents); the M/ARC-specific policy remains a proposal.

Structured claims should reference a fact with metric, entity, period, unit, data freshness and source type. The validator should detect “bench 100 kg” cited to “squat 100 kg,” current week cited to last week, a forecast described as observed, or “sleep caused the stall” supported only by co-occurrence. Important prescription numbers should be rendered directly from validated values. Repairs can resolve unsupported wording, but a failed prescription check should fall back to a safe known action rather than trusting an unverified new load.

Task-specific routing should favor local answers when the user needs a timer, next target, equipment rung or ordinary app navigation. Use a brief model pass for a short explanation; use deeper planning only for competing goals or programme revision. Cache stable evidence and invalidate on meaningful mutations. Before choosing a cheaper/faster provider or increasing model effort, measure correctness and useful-action latency on the same fixtures; a brand name is not the acceptance test.

Today, chat/plan/live receive the whole tool set, brief receives read tools, and the other one-shot modes receive no tools ([escobar-worker/src/anthropic.ts:119-139](https://github.com/macdarenz-droid/M-arc/blob/d5ebc771b3194dc557d469e40945dfa2ddf1f8ab/escobar-worker/src/anthropic.ts#L119-L139)). Eight small tools use strict schemas ([src/escobar/tools/schema.ts:109-115](https://github.com/macdarenz-droid/M-arc/blob/d5ebc771b3194dc557d469e40945dfa2ddf1f8ab/src/escobar/tools/schema.ts#L109-L115)); larger tools rely on local validation. Prefer task-scoped subsets and carefully constrained critical actions. Do not blindly turn every tool strict: schema support and combined-complexity limits must be checked against the chosen provider. Even perfect schema compliance does not establish that a chosen exercise or recommendation is appropriate. See the current [strict-tool contract](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/strict-tool-use) and [schema limits](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs).

## Memory contract

| Information | Proposed behavior | Required QA |
|---|---|---|
| “I prefer dumbbells” | Save as an explicit preference when the memory policy permits; use it as a preference, not a ban on everything else | Correction and forget take effect across chats and future proposals |
| “The rack is busy today” | Keep a session-scoped constraint | Tomorrow's plan is not permanently rewritten |
| “For the next month, protect this programme” | Apply a dated agreement, with clear ending/review semantics | No silent extension; no earlier override from generic optimization |
| “Maybe my sleep caused that bad set” | Preserve uncertainty if retained at all | Never summarize speculation as an established cause |
| Escobar proposes a change | Keep proposal status separate from acceptance | A suggestion does not become “you decided” without Apply/explicit agreement |
| User corrects or deletes a source record | Invalidate dependent summaries and decisions | An obsolete summary cannot reintroduce a deleted weight, injury or preference as a current fact |

Existing conversations, durable memory, derived summaries, raw workout data and caches need separate lifecycle rules. “Forget this” must have a clearly defined scope; if transcript removal is a distinct action, make that distinction discoverable. This is a design requirement, not a claim that current memory deletion redacts historical prose. Store no hidden psychographic or medical diagnosis inferred from app usage.

## Knowledge contract

Use a maintained evidence record for each coaching rule: claim, source, publication/review date, studied population, outcome, limits, applicability and the app decision it supports. The data provenance of a personal fact is different from the literature supporting a general principle. A valid citation to a user's load does not validate a claim about optimal hypertrophy.

Start with the existing curated knowledge base and review rules implicated by the audits. Add retrieval over approved content before giving the assistant unrestricted external research. The build/research artifacts should preserve citations even if current owner policy keeps citation markers out of the ordinary app answer. Any new in-app source UI should respect that policy rather than silently reversing it.

When research is incomplete or conflicting, Escobar should explain the practical tradeoff and avoid invented certainty. An evidence refresh should create a versioned, reviewable knowledge change with regression cases. It should not silently retrain personal recommendations or promote every new paper into a rule. Untrusted documents, exercise names and photo text are data; they cannot alter tool permissions or system policy.

## Fair comparison

Use comparisons that isolate different questions:

1. **Current versus proposed Escobar, same model:** hold model, inputs and scenario constant to test the value of context, tools and workflow changes.
2. **Candidate models, same improved app:** hold prompts, permitted data, tools and budgets as comparable as possible. Test API compatibility, refusals, latency and cost as well as task quality. Changing provider is not inherently an upgrade.
3. **General assistant, equivalent facts:** compare answer quality on the same de-identified snapshot and user request. Score advice and reasoning separately from app actions the generic assistant cannot perform.
4. **General assistant, equivalent tool adapter where feasible:** compare state-changing tasks only after equalizing tool access and authorization. Otherwise report the integration advantage explicitly.
5. **User workflow comparison:** measure time and corrections needed to finish a real gym task. This can show a product advantage even when base-model reasoning is the same.

Freeze model/version/date and preserve evaluation inputs. Blind judges to product/model identity where possible. Never claim broad superiority from a few favorable examples or combine unequal information access into a model-intelligence score.

|---|---|---|

| Unknown/out-of-scope or ambiguous health issue | Honest uncertainty, appropriate scope and escalation; no diagnosis | Critical-case rubric with zero tolerated severe errors in release set |
| Offline/quota/stop/reset during response | Local core remains usable; canceled or obsolete reply cannot commit | Async interleaving suite |

Quality should be judged by task completion, factual and action correctness, preserved user intent, correction rate, explanation usefulness, and time to a useful answer. Daily chat volume and message count are weak goals: an assistant that saves time may produce fewer messages. Track useful acceptance separately from blind acceptance; a user declining an inappropriate proposal is a safety success but a recommendation-quality failure.

Run three layers: deterministic/property checks for calculators, constraints and mutations; recorded model-output replay for orchestration and regressions; a held-out live-model evaluation with blind human grading and model-judge assistance. Compare the upgraded Escobar with current Escobar and a general chatbot given equivalent information. Keep prompt-training cases separate from the final evaluation set; report task mix, sample size, confidence intervals, errors, latency and cost rather than a single ungrounded “9/10.” More complete data access can be a product advantage, but it does not establish a stronger base model.

## Evaluation design

No new live-model evaluation or device test was run for these proposals. The following is a proposed QA programme for the responsible builder and reviewer.

**Initial scenario bank:** 120 authored cases across eight families, 15 per family: goal/constraint handling; load/mode/equipment; calendar/time; evidence/claim grounding; memory/longitudinal changes; proposals/Undo; privacy/cancellation; ambiguous input/offline/photo boundaries. Reserve five cases per family as a first held-out set and keep variants of the same person/trajectory together to avoid leakage. These counts are an initial engineering plan, not a power calculation or sufficient proof of rare-event safety.

Each fixture specifies the initial state revision, request, permitted data/actions, required facts, forbidden assumptions, acceptable alternatives, resulting state and allowed clarification. There may be several correct answers. A reasoning test should not force one exact phrase or one rigid tool order when another valid route reaches the same justified outcome.

Run deterministic state/constraint checks first. Then test recorded model responses through the orchestration layer. When the owner authorizes paid evaluations, run held-out cases on actual candidate models, repeat nondeterministic cases, and record final persisted state as well as replies. This outcome-based design is informed by [tau-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045) and [Anthropic’s evaluation guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents). Include both sampled failure cases and ordinary high-frequency tasks so the evaluation is neither unrealistically easy nor only adversarial.

| Evaluation layer | Primary measure | Release interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Data and actions | Correct IDs/units/periods, no lost committed work, expected state after Apply/Undo/reload | Any known violation blocks that feature; prose quality cannot compensate |
| Privacy and control | No new gated request, follow-up transmission or stale result commit after Off/cancel/reset; consent and memory semantics hold | Abort in-flight work where possible; data already transmitted cannot be recalled. Critical failures block release; zero failures in a finite test set is not a universal guarantee |
| Claims | Correct metric/entity/time/unit; no unsupported causal or physiological conclusion | Measure correctness and coverage separately so answering nothing cannot win |
| Goal fit | Honors explicit priorities and constraints; asks only material questions | Human rubric with concrete examples and adjudication of disagreements |
| Feasibility | Plan respects available equipment, time estimate bounds, protected work and rest policy | Invalid option is rejected before Apply; uncertain duration is not falsely guaranteed |
| Longitudinal behavior | Corrections, changing goals, expired constraints and summaries remain coherent | Test multi-week trajectories, sparse logs and backfilled history |
| Usefulness | Chosen task completed, fewer necessary corrections, appropriate detail | Compare against current app and a reasonable no-change option |
| Performance | p50/p95 time to first useful result and final result, failures and cost per successful task by mode | Set budgets after baseline/device measurement; live logging never waits for model output |
| Burden | Unwanted interventions, opt-outs, dismissal reasons, user-perceived control | More opens/messages is not a substitute for benefit |

Set release thresholds before inspecting candidate results. Keep critical contract failures as vetoes; use confidence intervals and case-level error reports for quality comparisons. Repeated runs of the same case are correlated and must not be presented as independent users. Train/tune graders on a separate set; calibrate model judges against human judgments and use code for objectively checkable outcomes. Private user traces are not uploaded by default; begin with synthetic fixtures.

Use a small formative usability study to uncover confusing workflows, then a limited consented pilot to examine task success and burden. A longer controlled comparison is needed before claiming better adherence or physical outcomes. A micro-randomized prompt study can test whether a particular message helps at a decision point; it cannot by itself prove that Escobar improves long-term strength.

## Performance policy

The local app remains responsible for fast logging, timers, basic targets and validated calculations. Invoke deeper reasoning for actual tradeoffs or investigation, not every screen redraw or set completion. Reuse revision-matched derived results, batch independent reads, and limit serial model/tool rounds. Model effort is a tunable behavior setting, not a guarantee of correctness or a hard spend cap.

Measure cost per successful user job using actual usage: model calls, uncached/cached input, output, retrieval and retries. Separate background generation from user-requested work. Quota admission and cancellation must be reliable before proactive generation increases call volume. No dollar estimate is supplied because the deployed configuration, usage mix and measured token consumption were not verified here.

Broader model routing or multiple cooperating agents should be introduced only when a controlled evaluation shows a useful gain over the simpler path. For example, a second review pass might improve a complex week-plan comparison; it is unlikely to justify delaying a known local rest timer. That is a testable design hypothesis, not a blanket ban or promised benefit.

## Builder handoff

The first deliverable should be a shared-facts/action-integrity foundation plus a small goal-and-constraint contract. The first user-facing experiment should be a brief daily decision and a reliable short-session replan, both backed by the same engine. Expand to weekly repair and outcome follow-through only after correctness and usefulness are demonstrated. Add voice or larger model budgets after measuring whether input friction or reasoning failures are the real limiting factor.

Preserve existing owner decisions about concise copy, hidden source markers and explicit Apply. Do not treat this proposal as approval to add new saved/sent data, a paid provider, a background service or a Worker deployment. No current claim of GPT/Claude parity is supported: the realistic target is better completion of the user's gym tasks through validated personal context and reliable app actions.
