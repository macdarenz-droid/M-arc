## What adaptation currently does

The current app already has substantial local adaptation: goal and exercise-role rep ranges; session-history target selection; equipment-aware load menus; re-entry and lighter-week behavior; readiness with personal check-in/resting-HR baselines and corroboration rules; per-muscle recovery calibration with bounded decay; working-set and fidelity filters; bodyweight-aware volume; schedule displacement/early-completion planning; user days off and planned-session targets; weekly effort/volume/weight/adherence insights; and feedback-based insight hiding/ranking. These are implemented mechanisms, not evidence that each prediction is accurate for a particular user.

The numerical recovery model combines effort, novelty, role weights, age/training age, dose stacking, sleep/load context and learned tau scales. The model's exact percentage, ready threshold, full threshold and ready-time range are internally chosen quantities. `confidenceFor` increases with observation count; count alone does not demonstrate calibration or predictive accuracy. No prospective validation dataset for these outputs was established in this review.

## Builder priorities for useful personalization

1. **Make measurement consistent first.** Address B1–B4 before adding more adaptation. Training completion, meaningful progress units, usable signal windows and consistent historical context are prerequisites for trustworthy personal advice.
2. **Separate intent from inference.** Store the user's explicit desired training outcome and weight direction independently; represent schedule availability, exercise preferences and temporary constraints distinctly from inferred habits. Any new kind of stored personal data requires the owner's existing data-shape approval process. Do not silently decide that every “lean muscle” user wants weight loss.
3. **Attach provenance to decisions.** Each proposed adjustment should have the exact source sessions, date window, relevant measured fields, confidence limitations, policy version and a reason it beat alternative actions. Match evidence text to those records; B5 shows why a fluent explanation is insufficient.
4. **Learn from comparable outcomes.** Assess repeated comparable exercises and sessions, separating load, reps, distance, time, equipment, effort and fidelity. Handle missed weeks, first exposure, long gaps, sparse sensors and edited logs as distinct cases. Avoid treating multiple pairs sharing one reference set as independent observations.
5. **Evaluate offline before expanding automation.** Use replayable synthetic invariants plus chronological train/validation splits on authorized, de-identified outcomes. Measure prediction error, calibration, abstention rates, false alerts and adaptation stability across users/goals. Compare against a simple history-only baseline. Keep an abstain/repeat option when data are sparse or contradictory.
6. **Keep the decision loop accountable.** Record acceptance, dismissal and subsequent performance for an adjustment only within authorized data boundaries. Avoid optimizing notification or suggestion volume as a success measure. Useful outcomes are adherence to the user's own plan, understandable recommendations and stable performance without oscillating prescriptions.

These proposals do not require a new paid model/provider. More expressive AI cannot repair inconsistent underlying measurements. The current evidence pipeline should be strengthened before attributing physiological certainty to more elaborate explanations.
