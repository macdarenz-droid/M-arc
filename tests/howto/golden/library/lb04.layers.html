Grip

Your shoulders hold the bar, hands loose round it, elbows high. Or cross your arms over it, elbows high.

Common handling mistakes

  • Chest falling, elbows dropping

    Fix: Elbows up, chest tall.

  • Hands holding the bar up

    Fix: Let your shoulders carry the bar. Try a fingertip rack.

  • Bailing with no safeties set

    Fix: Set the safeties. Let go, drop it forward onto them, step back.

Where you should feel it

You should feel this in your front thighs. Go as deep as balance and a neutral spine allow.

Set it up

  1. 1

    Set safeties just below your lowest depth. Heavy? Use a spotter.

  2. 2

    Bar between throat and shoulders. Shoulders pushed forward and slightly up.

  3. 3

    Loose full grip, elbows high. Sit straight down, knees and hips together.

  4. 4

    Heels hip width or a little wider, toes out 15-30 degrees.

  5. 5

    Walk out one step back on each leg. Brace hard each rep.

Risks and when to stop

  • A failed rep with no safeties set. Practise bailing light.
  • Holding your breath under heavy load raises blood pressure.
  • Bar pressing on the throat.

Wrist pain

Can't grip, wrist changed shape, or hand gone numb? Get it checked today.

Tingling, keeps coming back, or no better after two weeks' rest? See a doctor.

Back pain

Pain, numbness, tingling or weakness in both legs? Numb genitals or anus? Bladder, bowel or sexual changes? Chest pain? Serious accident? Get emergency help now. Sudden severe or fast-worsening pain? Hot, cold, shivery or unwell? See a doctor today.

No better in a few weeks? See a doctor.

Knee pain

Severe pain, badly swollen, changed shape, locking, giving way, or can't put weight on it? Get it checked today.

No better after a few weeks? See a doctor.

Set it up

  1. 1

    Stand with feet together, shoulders down and back, trunk braced.

  2. 2

    Balance on one foot, step forward, land heel first.

  3. 3

    Move the weight over slowly, upper body level.

  4. 4

    Drop the hips until the front thigh is about parallel.

  5. 5

    Push off firmly with the front leg to stand back up.

Risks and when to stop

  • Losing balance on the step. Learn single-leg standing first.
  • Uncontrolled forward shin travel when the hips drive forward.
