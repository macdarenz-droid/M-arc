Grip

Palms cradle the top plate, thumbs overlapping behind the handle. Let it hang vertically, not tilted flat by the wrists.

Common handling mistakes

  • Elbows bending into triceps extension

    Fix: Keep the same slight bend all the way.

  • Hips rising off the bench

    Fix: Dig your heels in, brace, stop where shoulder range ends.

  • Letting go too early

    Fix: Grip until the spotter has it or it rests on your thigh.

Where you should feel it

You should feel this as a stretch in your lats and lower chest.

Set it up

  1. 1

    Head as far up the bench as possible, feet flat.

  2. 2

    Keep five points of contact. Head neutral, eyes on the ceiling.

  3. 3

    Take the dumbbell from a spotter, or lift it from your thigh.

  4. 4

    Cradle the top plate, thumbs overlapping behind the handle. Grip it securely.

  5. 5

    Dumbbell above your upper chest, arms vertical, elbows slightly bent.

Risks and when to stop

  • Dumbbell over the face: grip it securely and use a spotter.
  • In US hospital data, dropped weights are the top cause of lifting injuries.
  • Small weight jumps load the shoulders hard. Start light, add weight in small steps.

Shoulder pain

Sudden or severe, after falling, or arm won't move? Get it checked today.

Getting worse, very hard to move, or no better after two weeks? See a doctor.

Elbow pain

Severe pain and hard to move, heard a snap, changed shape, tingling, or numbness? Get it checked today.

No better after a few weeks? See a doctor.

General guidance, not medical advice. If something hurts, stop and get it checked.
