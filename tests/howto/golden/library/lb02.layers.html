Grip

Closed grip, palms toward your feet. Bar in the palm's base, thumb wrapped.

Bar over the wrist, wrist not bent back.

Common handling mistakes

  • Pressing straight up off chest

    Fix: Drive it up and slightly back toward your shoulders.

  • Thumbless grip

    Fix: Wrap your thumbs round the bar.

  • Hips lifting, low back over-arched

    Fix: Buttocks down, feet pressing the floor. Lifted hips fail the rep.

Where you should feel it

You should feel this in your mid-chest, front shoulders and the backs of your arms.

    Set the safety pins slightly below where the bar touches your chest.

  2. 2

    Shoulders at least 8 cm from uprights. Feet flat. Blades together.

  3. 3

    Grip slightly wider than shoulders, thumbs wrapped, bar low in the palm.

  4. 4

Risks and when to stop

  • Bar over face and neck: wrap the thumbs. Use safety pins or a spotter.
  • Pec tendon rupture while lowering the bar.
  • Weights dropping on the lifter are a leading cause of weight-training injuries.

Common handling mistakes

Wrong

Right: Arms nearly straight, same slight bend all the way.

Wrong: Elbows deeply bent, like a skull crusher.

Keep your head, blades and hips on the bench and both feet down.

Grip

Palms cradle the top plate, thumbs overlapping behind the handle. Let it hang vertically, not tilted flat by the wrists.

Common handling mistakes

  • Elbows bending into triceps extension

    Fix: Keep the same slight bend all the way.

  • Hips rising off the bench

    Fix: Dig your heels in, brace, stop where shoulder range ends.

  • Letting go too early

    Fix: Grip until the spotter has it or it rests on your thigh.

Where you should feel it

You should feel this as a stretch in your lats and lower chest.

MainAlso workingShould not take overWhere it may hurt
If you feel it in…

  • Usually meansBending and straightening the elbows can move work to the triceps.

    FixKeep the elbow bend the same.

  • Usually meansToo much bend with flared elbows may stress the inner elbow.

    FixStop and don't train through the pain. Keep elbows pointing forward or up.

  • FixStop the set. Don't train through the pain.

Set it up

  1. 1

    Head as far up the bench as possible, feet flat.

  2. 2

    Keep five points of contact. Head neutral, eyes on the ceiling.

  3. 3

    Take the dumbbell from a spotter, or lift it from your thigh.

  4. 4

    Cradle the top plate, thumbs overlapping behind the handle. Grip it securely.

  5. 5

    Dumbbell above your upper chest, arms vertical, elbows slightly bent.

Risks and when to stop

  • Dumbbell over the face: grip it securely and use a spotter.
  • Weights dropping on the lifter are a leading cause of weight-training injuries.
  • Small weight jumps load the shoulders hard. Start light, add weight in small steps.
